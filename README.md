# AmitySpace

[Our Website](https://amityspace.online/)

## Introduction

AmitySpace is a digital platform designed to support parents, caregivers, and family members of autistic individuals when planning community outings in Melbourne. Our project focuses on making public spaces easier to understand by presenting sensory-related information in a simple and practical way. By helping users make more informed decisions before they go out, AmitySpace aims to reduce stress, support inclusion, and encourage more confident participation in community life.

## Problem Statement

Parents, caregivers, and family members of autistic individuals aged under 18 to 35 in Melbourne face a significant challenge when planning and accompanying community outings because many public spaces meant for social connection, such as parks, libraries, museums, community centres, and busy pedestrian areas, contain invisible sensory barriers. These spaces can involve loud noise, harsh lighting, crowding, and unpredictable stimulation, which can make participation overwhelming or unsustainable for autistic individuals. This problem occurs across public and cultural spaces in Melbourne where people usually go to socialise, attend events, and build community ties. Because these sensory barriers are often unmapped and overlooked in current accessibility planning and urban design, autistic individuals may avoid community participation not because they lack interest, but because the sensory cost is too high, leading to repeated withdrawal, isolation, and lower well-being.

How might we help people around autistic adults make safer and more confident decisions about community outings, by making the sensory conditions of public spaces in Melbourne CBD visible, predictable, and easier to plan around?

## Target Audience

Parents, caregivers, and family members of autistic individuals (under 18 - 35) in Melbourne who plan and accompany community outings to public spaces (such as parks, libraries, museums, and community centres) in the CBD and inner suburbs.

Including two key sub-groups:
- Parents/guardians of autistic children (under 18), who are the primary decision-makers for their child's community participation
- Family members (parents, siblings, partners) and friends of autistic young adults (18-35) who are transitioning out of structured school or NDIS support and rely on family assistance to navigate unfamiliar public environments
